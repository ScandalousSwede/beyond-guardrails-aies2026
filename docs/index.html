Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate sensitive domains, including mental health, identity, and meaning-making. While recent safety interventions have reduced well-documented harms, they have also introduced new and less visible failure patterns. This paper proposes a dual failure-mode framework for LLM safety behaviour, framed through a control-systems lens. Mode 1 (sycophantic validation) arises from runaway positive feedback, in which models overvalidate user beliefs without sufficient damping, amplifying distress or distorted premises. Mode 2 (rigid pathologization) arises from excessive negative feedback, in which models assert epistemic authority and categorically invalidate or suppress culturally, spiritually, or identity-grounded frameworks through overly narrow evaluative criteria. We argue that both modes share a common mechanism: misplaced epistemic authority, where models act as arbiters of reality rather than contextual collaborators exercising calibrated epistemic humility.
